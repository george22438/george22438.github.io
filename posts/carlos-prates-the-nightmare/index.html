Carlos Prates: The Nightmare

George Garcia · May 23, 2026

In a world of manufactured stars and highlight reel athletes, Carlos Prates moves like a ghost from the streets, raw, unpredictable, and dangerously alive. He is my current favorite fighter because he somehow carries both the soul of the common man and the precision of an elite striker in the same scarred pair of gloves.

Prates fights like poetry written in violence. There is a Ricardo Mayorga type chaos in him that wild, rebellious spirit that refuses to be tamed yet it’s married to something far more refined. His left straight, delivered from his skinny frame, lands like a Jorge Linares sniper shot: sudden, surgical, and devastating. It doesn’t look like it should carry that kind of power, but when it lands, it rewrites the night.

What separates him is his patience inside the storm. While others rush forward with reckless aggression, Prates stalks. He waits. He reads. Then, when the moment reveals itself, he strikes with the calmness of a man who has already seen the ending in his mind. His striking isn’t just technical it’s artistic. He flows between combinations like jazz, switching stances, mixing entries, and creating angles that feel improvised yet calculated. One moment he’s a southpaw pressure fighter, the next he’s circling like a traditional boxer, only to explode into a flying knee or a spinning elbow when the opponent least expects it.

Controlled chaos. He carries the slums in his heart and the spotlight on his shoulders without letting either define him completely. The long years of grinding, of hammering away in obscurity, feel like a man working construction in the dead of night, swinging steel while the city sleeps, waiting for the foreman to finally step down. That main event walkout, cigarette in mouth after the victory, is more than celebration. It’s a quiet “… Fuck you…” to every system that tried to keep him below the ladder, while still honoring the streets that raised him.

His free spirit breathes through every fight. You can see it in the way he refuses to be patterned. Opponents come in with strategies. Prates comes in with feeling. He fights like a man painting with blood and leather, every strike an expression, every exchange a verse in a story only he can tell.

This is why Carlos Prates is my favorite fighter right now. In him, I see the perfect marriage of darkness and glory, hunger and artistry, rebellion and discipline. He doesn’t just win fights, he reminds us why we fall in love with this brutal, beautiful sport in the first place.
