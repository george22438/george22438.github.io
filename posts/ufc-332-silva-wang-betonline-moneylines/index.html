UFC 332 Silva vs. Wang: BetOnline Moneylines + Fight-by-Fight Analysis

George Garcia · Oct 2, 2026

UFC 332: Silva vs. Wang: Saturday, October 3, 2026, Delta Center, Salt Lake City, Utah. Early prelims 1:00 PM PT (Paramount+ / UFC Fight Pass), prelims 3:00 PM PT, main card 5:00 PM PT on CBS and Paramount+. It's the first UFC numbered main card to air on CBS. The vacant women's flyweight title is on the line in a five-round main event; everything else is scheduled for three.

BetOnline American moneylines come first, then the card fight by fight: who each fighter is, what they're good at, and how they actually win. Favorites in red, underdogs in green. Records and finish numbers come from UFC.com athlete pages; weigh-in status comes from UFC.com, MMA Fighting and Sherdog. Where a file is thin (debuts, long layoffs), I say so.

Main card

Natalia Silva (−207) vs Wang Cong (+175), vacant women's flyweight title, 5 rounds

Silva (20-5-1, #1 flyweight): Silva hasn't lost in 14 straight fights and is a perfect 8-0 in the UFC. Her last three wins came over Rose Namajunas, Alexa Grasso and Jéssica Andrade. Her base is taekwondo. She fights long and light on her feet, kicks from both sides, and lands clean shots without taking many back. She's also a jiu-jitsu brown belt, and six of her seven career submissions are armbars. Over her career she has 5 KO/TKO wins and 7 submissions. In the UFC, though, she wins by decision: her last five were all unanimous.

Wang (10-1, #8): Wang is a technical striker. UFC.com credits her with the highest significant-strike accuracy in women's flyweight history (58.2%), and she owns a 2015 pro kickboxing win over Valentina Shevchenko. She's won four straight, all decisions, over Tracy Cortez, Eduarda Moura, Ariane da Silva and Bruna Brasil. Her one loss came by rear-naked choke against Gabriella Fernandes. Career finishes: 2 KO/TKO, 2 submissions, 6 decisions.

How it plays out: Silva's path is the one she's used all along: move, kick, win the rounds on volume and variety, and take the armbar if Wang gets careless on the mat. Wang wins by being the more accurate puncher at close range, timing Silva coming in and banking close rounds. Neither woman has gone five rounds in the UFC, so the championship rounds are a real unknown. Wang's only loss was on the ground, and that's a problem against someone with Silva's armbar record.

Pick: Silva by decision. −207 is a fair price; I wouldn't lay much more than that.

Payton Talbott (−800) vs Deiveson Figueiredo (+550), bantamweight

Talbott (11-1): A rangy (70.5" reach) striker out of Reno who switches stances, mixes up angles and has knockout power. Seven of his 11 wins are KO/TKO, including a 19-second finish of Yanis Ghemmouri. He's also shown he can go 15 minutes, with decision wins over Felipe Lima and Henry Cejudo his last two times out. His one loss was a decision to Raoni Barcelos.

Figueiredo (25-7-1, #9 bantamweight): The former two-time flyweight champion is 38 now and fighting at 135. His finishes are split evenly: 9 KO/TKO and 9 submissions, five of them guillotines. Recent form is the concern. Since late 2024 he's lost a five-rounder to Petr Yan, a TKO (injury) to Cory Sandhagen, a decision to Umar Nurmagomedov and, in May, a guillotine to Song Yadong. The one win in that stretch was a split decision over Montel Jackson.

How it plays out: Talbott wants this at range, where his speed and length make Figueiredo reach. Figueiredo wins with a heavy right hand early or by catching a neck when Talbott changes levels or scrambles. He's still a real guillotine threat.

Pick: Talbott, most likely late KO or decision. At −800 he's parlay filler, not a straight bet.

Esteban Ribovics (−215) vs King Green (+183), lightweight (catchweight)

Ribovics (16-3): An Argentine power striker out of Kill Cliff FC with 8 KO/TKO and 5 submissions, three of them kimuras. He has five wins in 90 seconds or less, and his 2024 fight with Daniel Zellhuber was one of the wildest of the year. In August he stopped Edson Barboza in the second round; in April, Mateusz Gamrot submitted him with an arm-triangle. This is his third fight of 2026 and his second in 49 days. He missed weight by half a pound.

Green (36-17-1): At 40, the former Bobby Green is on a four-fight win streak: a split decision over Lance Gibson Jr., then stoppages of Daniel Zellhuber (TKO), Jeremy Stephens (RNC) and Terrance McKinney (TKO). He's a slick, hands-low counter boxer who talks to you while he slips punches, and he has 13 KO/TKO and 10 submission wins. The risk on his side is that the big punchers have stopped him: Mauricio Ruffy, Jalin Turner and Drew Dober all knocked him out.

How it plays out: Ribovics wins by pushing a hard pace and landing heavy shots in exchanges. Green wins by making him miss, countering, and grinding the rounds out with his boxing, or by catching a tired Ribovics late. The quick turnaround plus the miss on the scale is something to watch.

Pick: Ribovics, but Green at +183 is the best underdog price on the main card given how he's been fighting.

Roberto Soldić (−200) vs Khaos Williams (+169), welterweight

Soldić (21-4, UFC debut): The former two-division KSW champion ("Robocop") finally makes his UFC debut. Eighteen of his 21 wins are by knockout, and he's only gone to decision four times in 26 pro fights. He split two KSW title fights with Dricus Du Plessis, one knockout each way. He grew up in judo and is 4-0 (4 KOs) as a pro boxer. Data caution: this is his first MMA fight in 590 days (last one February 2025), and he's coming back down to welterweight after four fights at middleweight. UFC.com has no Octagon stats for him yet.

Williams (16-5): A power puncher out of Murcielago MMA with 9 KO/TKO wins, including two Octagon knockouts in under 30 seconds (Morono, Alhassan). In May he got back on track with a first-round TKO of Nikolay Veretennikov, after a decision loss to Andreas Gustafsson and a D'Arce choke loss to Gabriel Bonfim.

How it plays out: Both men want to land the big shot. Soldić is the more complete fighter, with combination punching and the grappling to change things up. Williams wins if Soldić is rusty early and gets caught exchanging in the first round.

Pick: Soldić by KO/TKO, with the layoff as the reason to keep the bet small.

Ateba Gautier (−230) vs Roman Kopylov (+193), middleweight

Gautier (11-1): A 24-year-old from Cameroon training at Manchester Top Team, with an 81" reach and a 10-fight win streak. Nine of his 11 wins are knockouts and he has no submissions. He's 5-0 in the UFC with four KOs. The one fight that went the distance was a decision over Andrey Pulyaev in January; he followed it with a second-round KO of Ozzy Diaz in May.

Kopylov (15-5): A pure striker. All 12 of his finishes are KO/TKO, often late (five second-round and four third-round finishes, plus a third-round head-kick KO of Chris Curtis). His five losses all came in the UFC and none were by knockout: two submissions (Karl Roberson, Anthony Hernandez) and three decisions. He's coming off a decision win over Marco Tulio.

How it plays out: Gautier wants to keep Kopylov at the end of that 81-inch reach and punish him when he steps in. Kopylov's best chance is to survive the early power, use his durability and patience, and build toward a late finish the way he usually does.

Pick: Gautier. Kopylov's chin makes a decision more likely than the price suggests.

Prelims

Imanol Rodriguez (−135) vs Alden Coria (+115), flyweight

Rodriguez (7-0): A puncher from Ensenada with 6 KO/TKO wins and one submission. He's 2-0 as a pro boxer, made the semifinals of TUF 33 (that loss doesn't count on his pro record), knocked out Roque Conceição on the Contender Series, and stopped Kevin Borjas in his UFC debut in February.

Coria (13-3): A freestyle fighter with a balanced record of 5 KO/TKO, 4 submissions and 4 decisions. He's 3-0 in the UFC (TKO of Alessandro Costa, then decisions over Luis Gurule and Stewart Nicoll). He has the reach edge, 67" to 64.5".

How it plays out: Rodriguez wants exchanges in the pocket where his power shows up. Coria wins by using his length, mixing in takedowns, and keeping it a technical three-round fight.

Pick: Coria at plus money. He's been more proven at this level, but it's a close fight.

Damian Pinas (−600) vs Andrey Pulyaev (+425), middleweight

Pinas (10-1): A 24-year-old knockout artist from Suriname fighting out of Aruba, with 9 KO/TKO wins (eight in the first round) and one D'Arce choke. His only loss was a 2024 DQ for an illegal kick. He's 2-0 in the UFC with two first-round stoppages.

Pulyaev (10-5): Big and long for the division (6'4", 78.5" reach), with 6 KO/TKO and 2 submission wins. He's 1-3 in the UFC: decision losses to Christian Leroy Duncan and Ateba Gautier, a TKO of Nick Klein, and a first-round rear-naked choke loss to Nursulton Ruziboev in June.

How it plays out: Pinas wants a fast start and a first-round finish. Pulyaev needs to use his length, survive the early storm, and see if Pinas fades after a round he's rarely had to fight past.

Pick: Pinas by first-round KO/TKO.

Marcus McGhee (−480) vs Anthony Romero (+374), featherweight

McGhee (11-2): A 36-year-old MMA Lab product with 8 KO/TKO wins and a BJJ base. He's 5-1 in the UFC, with finishes of Journey Newson, JP Buys and Gaston Bolanos and decisions over Jonathan Martinez and John Yannis. The one loss was a decision to Petr Yan. He has been grinding out decisions more lately.

Romero (7-2, UFC debut): A Blackhouse MMA fighter and the reigning UP Next Fighting champion, with 4 KO/TKO and 2 submission wins, two of them in under a minute. Data caution: regional-only résumé and no UFC stats.

How it plays out: McGhee's experience, power and grappling give him several ways to win. Romero wins by landing early, before the step up in competition shows.

Pick: McGhee, by decision or a mid-fight stoppage.

Anthony Wint (−550) vs Lucas Armand (+415), heavyweight

Wint (8-0): A former FIU linebacker who spent time with the New York Jets, with 5 KO/TKO and 2 submissions. He won his Contender Series fight in 34 seconds and then won his UFC debut on August 22 with a first-round arm-triangle on Terrance Chatman. He's an athletic wrestler who can finish on the mat or on the feet, and he's back on about six weeks' rest.

Armand (6-0, UFC debut): 6'2.5" and about 265 lbs, from Murcielago MMA. He won his pro debut by decision and has knocked out every opponent since (5 straight KO/TKOs), coming off three title fights in Michigan's 2 Piece Promotions. Data caution: regional competition only.

How it plays out: Wint wins by mixing in takedowns and going for an early finish. Armand's path is to stay standing and land one clean shot, which always matters at heavyweight.

Pick: Wint, by first-round finish or ground control.

Early prelims

Johnny Walker (−115) vs Mick Parkin (−105), heavyweight

Walker (22-10, 1 NC): One of the most explosive finishers in the sport: 17 KO/TKO wins, 17 first-round finishes, six finishes under a minute, and an 82" reach. Every UFC fight on his record so far has been at light heavyweight, so this heavyweight bout is new territory. Recent form: a split-decision loss to Dominick Reyes in April, a TKO of Zhang Mingyang before that, and KO losses to Volkan Oezdemir, Magomed Ankalaev and Jamahal Hill on his record.

Parkin (10-1): A big (6'4", around 260) heavyweight from Sunderland with a 7-0 amateur record. He has 6 KO/TKO wins and is 4-1 in the UFC, though most of those wins were decisions. His only loss was a decision to Marcin Tybura in March 2025. Data caution: he hasn't fought in roughly 18 months.

How it plays out: Walker wins in the first five to seven minutes with knees, kicks and wild power. Parkin wins by leaning his weight on Walker, wrestling, and dragging him into a longer fight, away from Walker's first-round danger zone.

Pick: True coin flip. Slight lean to Walker by early KO; this is the fight I'd most likely pass on.

Jacobe Smith (−1500) vs Bruce Whitehead (+700), welterweight

Smith (12-0): A two-time NJCAA national champion wrestler who hits hard. He has 9 KO/TKO wins, has finished six straight opponents, and is 3-0 in the UFC (Preston Parsons KO, Niko Price RNC, Josiah Harrell first-round KO in February).

Whitehead (8-2, UFC debut): A Houston firefighter who wrestled at Lyon College, with 2 KO/TKO and 3 submission wins, four of them in under a minute. All of his pro fights have been in Fury FC, and this is his second fight in 13 days. Data caution: regional-only record.

How it plays out: Smith wrestles and pressures him until the finish comes. Whitehead needs a fast scramble finish of his own.

Pick: Smith by stoppage. −1500 is not a straight bet.

Rafael dos Anjos (+222) vs Alexander Hernandez (−270), lightweight

Hernandez (18-9): A fast-starting, aggressive striker with 8 KO/TKO wins, 8 decisions and 8 first-round finishes. He stopped Chase Hooper and Diego Ferreira in 2025, then lost a decision to Rafa Garcia in April.

Dos Anjos (32-17): The former lightweight champion has 11 submissions, 5 KOs and 16 decisions. He's a pressure kickboxer with a black-belt ground game. Data caution: he's 41, hasn't fought since an October 2024 TKO (injury) loss to Geoff Neal, and has lost three straight (Vicente Luque, Mateusz Gamrot, Neal).

How it plays out: Hernandez wins by pushing the pace early and out-landing him. RDA wins by surviving the first round, then using pressure, leg kicks and top control.

Pick: Hernandez, most likely by decision.

Ismail Naurdiev (−136) vs Marvin Vettori (+116), middleweight

Naurdiev (25-8): Listed as a wrestler but finishes like a striker, with 13 KO/TKO and 6 submissions and 16 first-round finishes. He's 2-1 in his second UFC run: a decision over Bruno Silva, a decision loss to JunYong Park, and a first-round KO of Ryan Loder in November.

Vettori (19-10-1): The former title challenger has never been finished in his career. He's a high-volume, durable pressure fighter with 9 submissions and 8 decisions. He has dropped four straight, but all were decisions against top opposition (Jared Cannonier, Roman Dolidze, Brendan Allen, Brunno Ferreira).

How it plays out: Naurdiev wants to land early and threaten takedowns. Vettori wants a 15-minute fight decided by pace and volume.

Pick: Vettori at plus money, low confidence. His losses came against a much higher level of opponent than Naurdiev has faced.

Court McGee (+189) vs Eric Nolan (−225), welterweight

McGee (23-14): TUF 11 winner, Ogden native, 41 years old, and 2-0 in UFC fights in Utah. This is his final fight. He has 7 submissions, 5 KOs and 11 decisions, and he's a durable wrestler-grinder. His last fight was a decision loss to Michael Chiesa in June 2025. The Matt Brown and Jeremiah Wells knockouts are on his record.

Nolan (8-5): A retired U.S. Marine with 4 KO/TKO and 2 submission wins. He's 0-2 in the UFC (submitted by Baisangur Susurkaev, decision loss to Farman Hasanov). He has the youth and size edge.

How it plays out: Nolan wins by being the fresher, younger athlete and landing power. McGee wins by turning it into a 15-minute grind, which he's done throughout his career.

Pick: McGee at +189 as a value lean. That price feels too long for a guy whose opponent hasn't won in the UFC yet.

Data gaps

Four debutants (Soldić, Romero, Armand, Whitehead) have no UFC stats yet. Soldić is coming off a 590-day layoff, Parkin roughly 18 months, and Dos Anjos nearly two years. UFC.com's win-method breakdowns don't always add up to the posted record (Silva's, for example). Some recent-fight labels on UFC.com athlete pages are also wrong; where they conflicted, I used the written fight-by-fight results in each athlete's UFC.com bio. No bouts were scratched as of the snapshot.

Records and finish numbers from UFC.com athlete pages; weigh-in results via UFC.com, MMA Fighting and Sherdog. Odds from BetOnline.ag; lines move.
