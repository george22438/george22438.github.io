Josh Hokit Didn't Come Here to Play It Safe

George Garcia · Nov 12, 2026

Four UFC fights. That's all it took. Josh Hokit is 10-0, and on Saturday night at Madison Square Garden he walks into the main event of UFC 334 to fight Ciryl Gane for the undisputed heavyweight title. In a division that usually makes people wait, Hokit's incline has been almost vertical. He is one of the few fighters in the UFC right now making a real, unmistakable push for the heavyweight championship — and he has done it at a speed this sport is not used to seeing at heavyweight.

He takes the moment

Too often, fighters get timid when the microphones come out and the cameras start to flash. They shrink. They wait to be asked. Hokit does the opposite. He takes advantage of every single moment to pull the audience in. The behavior can look sporadic from the outside — loud, strange, a little unhinged. That is the point. The man is cerebral in the way he handles the pre-fight and post-fight noise. The antics are not accidents. They are a way of holding the room.

Then the cage door closes, and the calculation gets thrown out the window.

Inside the cage, he blitzes

Once he's locked in there, Hokit is a legit force. The planning that goes into the showmanship does not follow him into the first exchange. He looks to blitz people with volume and pace — an intense, constant pressure that most heavyweights simply do not throw. UFC.com's numbers back up what the eye already tells you: he lands 9.29 significant strikes per minute at 65% accuracy, and 91% of that work is aimed at the head. Six of his 10 wins are knockouts. Three are submissions. The only decision on the record is the one that announced him, a unanimous win over Curtis Blaydes in April.

The path here was short and violent. He earned his UFC contract on Dana White's Contender Series in August 2025, stopping Guilherme Uriel in the second round. He stopped Max Gimenis in 56 seconds in his November debut, finished Denzel Freeman in the first round in January, went the distance with Blaydes, and then knocked out Derrick Lewis in the second round on June 14 at UFC Freedom 250. A year ago he was still fighting for a contract. This Saturday he is fighting for the title.

The Lewis fight was the exception

Against Lewis, Hokit took his time and played the game a little safer. That is not the version I expect in New York.

I expect Hokit to blitz Gane and not allow him to float and jab around the cage. Gane's footwork is the best in the heavyweight division. His speed, and his ability to strike in and out of range, is unparalleled. He is 6'4" with an 81-inch reach, a technician who lands 5.40 significant strikes per minute and absorbs just 2.31, and he became undisputed champion after stopping Alex Pereira for the interim title in June and being promoted in September when Tom Aspinall vacated the belt (UFC.com). On paper, that is a problem for anybody who has to cross distance.

But Hokit has a kind of size and hand speed most heavyweights don't. He is listed at 6'1", 240 pounds, compact for the division, and he can get his hands off in a hurry. Perhaps Gane will find that style too chaotic and too unpredictable to settle into the rhythm he wants. BetOnline has Gane at −171 and Hokit at +150. That is a real underdog number, and it is also a number that can look thin if Hokit gets to him early.

The wrestling has to show up

For Hokit to be successful Saturday night in New York, he has to create level changes while he is blitzing. Mix the combinations to the head and to the body. In the midst of the brawl, if Gane happens to set his feet, Hokit should be looking for a single or a double and dragging the fight to the ground. Wrestling is still his best skill to lean on in this fight — he came up a California state champion at Clovis High and an All-American at Fresno State, and he trains out of Jackson Wink. Gane's takedown defense sits at 47%, the softest number on the champion's sheet.

If Hokit cannot wrestle Gane to the ground, I don't see him winning this fight. The blitz has to be real, but the level change is what makes the blitz dangerous. Without it, Gane gets to stay at the end of that 81-inch reach and jab the night away.

The pick

I am choosing Hokit to deliver the upset. And if he does it, he deserves to win fighter of the year — not as a courtesy, but because of the incredible speed it took him to earn a title shot, and then, maybe, to take hold of the title itself. From a Contender Series contract in August 2025 to a heavyweight championship fight at Madison Square Garden in November 2026 is a climb this division almost never allows. Saturday is the night we find out if the incline goes all the way to the top.
