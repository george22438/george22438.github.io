UFC 334 Gane vs. Hokit: BetOnline Moneylines + Fight-by-Fight Analysis

George Garcia · Nov 12, 2026

UFC 334: Gane vs. Hokit: Saturday, November 14, 2026, Madison Square Garden, New York. Early prelims 2:00 PM PT (Paramount+ and UFC Fight Pass), prelims 4:00 PM PT, main card 6:00 PM PT, all on Paramount+ in the U.S. Thirteen fights: five on the main card, four prelims and four early prelims. Two title fights headline the Garden: heavyweight champion Ciryl Gane defends against unbeaten Josh Hokit (No. 4 on UFC.com's event page), and bantamweight champion Kayla Harrison defends against the returning Amanda Nunes. Both are five rounds; everything else is three.

Main card

Ciryl Gane (−171) vs Josh Hokit (+150), heavyweight title, 5 rounds

Gane (14-2, 1 NC, age 36): "Bon Gamin" is the champion: he stopped Alex Pereira with strikes in the second round on June 14 to win the interim title and was promoted to undisputed champion in September after Tom Aspinall vacated (UFC.com). He's a 6'4" Muay Thai stylist out of MMA Factory in Paris with an 81" reach, and his numbers are a technician's: 5.40 significant strikes landed per minute against 2.31 absorbed, 62% accuracy and 61% strike defense. His attack is spread out like a kickboxer's: 45% of his significant strikes go to the head, 27% to the body and 29% to the legs, and 89% of his work happens standing. He has 7 KO/TKO wins, 3 submissions (guillotine, arm triangle, heel hook) and 4 decisions. The soft spot is the takedown: 47% takedown defense, and both losses came against elite opposition (a five-round decision to Francis Ngannou in 2022, a guillotine by Jon Jones in 2023). His October 2025 fight with Aspinall was a no contest after an accidental eye poke.

Hokit (10-0, age 28): A former Fresno State All-American wrestler (and a California state champion at Clovis High) who spent two seasons on the San Francisco 49ers' practice squad before turning pro in October 2023. He's 6'1", 240 lbs, with a 73.5" reach, out of Jackson Wink. UFC.com lists his style as Wrestling, but his UFC run has been a striking blitz: 9.29 significant strikes landed per minute at 65% accuracy, with 91% of them aimed at the head, and 6 of his 10 wins are KO/TKOs (plus 3 submissions and 1 decision). The cost is what comes back: 5.28 absorbed per minute and 49% strike defense. His takedown defense is 78%. UFC run: a 56-second stoppage of Max Gimenis in his November 2025 debut, a first-round TKO of Denzel Freeman in January, a unanimous decision over Curtis Blaydes in April, and a second-round knockout of Derrick Lewis on June 14.

How it plays out: Gane has a 7.5-inch reach advantage and the cleaner, more varied offense; he wants to kick from range, touch the body and make Hokit miss. Hokit's path is pressure and chaos: close the distance, force exchanges in the pocket, and mix in the wrestling threat against a champion whose takedown defense is the weakest number on his sheet. Five rounds favor the fighter with the better defense, but Hokit has never been past three. Chris Weidman is picking the upset (MMA News).

Pick: Gane by decision or late TKO. At +150, Hokit's early pressure is a live upset.

Kayla Harrison (−170) vs Amanda Nunes (+149), women's bantamweight title, 5 rounds

Harrison (19-1, age 36): A two-time Olympian in judo (UFC.com: Judo; her bio mentions her second Olympics in 2016) and the youngest American ever awarded a sixth-degree black belt, out of American Top Team. She's 3-0 in the UFC: an RNC of Holly Holm, a decision over Ketlen Vieira, then a second-round kimura of Julianna Peña to win the title at UFC 316 in June 2025; this is her first defense. The numbers are a control fighter's: 2.84 takedowns per 15 minutes, 0.95 submission attempts per 15, and 53% of her significant strikes land on the ground. She's also hard to hit, absorbing just 1.10 per minute with 66% strike defense, and nobody has taken her down in the UFC (100% takedown defense). She has 8 submissions (four armbars, arm triangle, keylock, kimura, RNC), 6 KO/TKO and 5 decisions. Her lone pro loss came before the UFC.

Nunes (23-5, age 38): "The Lioness" is the only woman to hold UFC titles in two divisions at once, with six defenses at 135 and two at 145 (UFC.com). She hasn't fought since a five-round decision over Irene Aldana in June 2023, after which she vacated the title, so she's coming off nearly two and a half years away. At her best she was a power striker with a wrestling base (UFC.com: Striker): 4.55 landed per minute, 52% accuracy, 58% strike defense, 82% takedown defense and 2.73 takedowns per 15 minutes. She has 13 KO/TKO wins, 4 submissions and 6 decisions, with 14 first-round finishes. Her last loss is the December 2021 RNC by Peña, which she avenged by decision in July 2022.

How it plays out: Nunes has the heavier hands and, on paper, the takedown defense to keep this standing; Harrison wants the clinch, a trip or throw, and top position. The layoff is the unknown: Nunes hasn't fought in 29 months against a champion who absorbs just 1.10 significant strikes per minute. If Nunes lands early, the line looks wrong; if Harrison gets to the body lock, it's her fight.

Pick: Harrison by decision or late submission.

Caio Borralho (−165) vs Yousri Belgaroui (+145), middleweight

Borralho (18-2, age 33): "The Natural," No. 5 on the event page, is a Fighting Nerds pressure fighter (UFC.com: MMA) with a 75" reach. He's patient and efficient rather than busy: 3.47 landed per minute against 2.61 absorbed, 56% accuracy, 60% strike defense and 76% takedown defense, with 79% of his work standing. He has 5 KO/TKO, 4 submissions (guillotine, anaconda, 2 RNC) and 9 decisions, so he's a decision fighter at this level. A five-round decision loss to Nassourdine Imavov in September 2025 snapped a 14-fight winning streak; he rebounded with a decision over Reinier de Ridder in March.

Belgaroui (11-3, age 34): A 6'6" Dutch kickboxer, the tallest middleweight on the roster per UFC.com, with a 79" reach and a 27-13 pro kickboxing record (three Glory title shots, two losses to Israel Adesanya and a 1-2 series with Alex Pereira, whom he now trains with at Teixeira MMA). In MMA he's a pure striker: 6.44 landed per minute at 63% accuracy, 64% strike defense, and 86% of his strikes come standing. He has 9 KO/TKO wins and 2 decisions, has never been finished, and is 3-0 in the UFC with three stoppages (Azamat Bekoev, Mansur Abdul-Malik, Djorden Santos in September).

How it plays out: Belgaroui has four inches of reach and the cleaner kickboxing; Borralho has the grappling, the pressure and far deeper UFC experience against ranked opposition. Borralho needs to mix takedowns into his entries; Belgaroui's 67% takedown defense hasn't been tested by anyone like him.

Pick: Borralho by decision. Belgaroui at +145 is a fair shot if it stays at kicking range.

Uroš Medić (−165) vs Kevin Holland (+145), welterweight

Medić (14-3, age 33): "The Doctor," No. 12 on the event page, is a Serbian finisher out of Anchorage BJJ, and possibly the most first-round-heavy fighter on the card: 12 KO/TKO wins, 2 submissions (both triangles), 0 decisions, and a 3.72 knockdown average per 15 minutes. He lands 5.77 per minute at 61% accuracy with 57% strike defense and 56% takedown defense. His recent run: knockouts of Muslim Salikhov (1:03) and Geoff Neal (1:19), then a 30-second finish of Daniel Rodriguez at UFC Belgrade on August 1. All three of his losses are first- or second-round finishes (Jalin Turner, Myktybek Orolbai, Punahele Soriano).

Holland (29-15, age 34): "Trailblazer," No. 15, is a 6'3" kung fu–based striker (UFC.com: Kung Fu) with an 81" reach, 10 inches longer than Medić's. He lands 4.24 per minute with 50% strike defense, kicks the legs a lot (27% of his significant strikes) and has a dangerous scramble game: 14 KO/TKO wins, 9 submissions (including two D'Arces) and 6 decisions. He's been up and down: a D'Arce of Vicente Luque in June 2025, decision losses to Daniel Rodriguez and Mike Malott, then a decision over Randy Brown in April.

How it plays out: Holland's reach and volume against Medić's one-shot power. Holland wants range, leg kicks and a long fight; Medić needs one clean exchange, and he has beaten the man who beat Holland in 30 seconds. Holland's chin and his submission scrambles are his insurance.

Pick: Medić by KO/TKO.

Bilal Hasan (−1000) vs Luis Gurule (+661), flyweight

Hasan (10-0, age 25): "The IndoNinja" represents Indonesia, trains with Demetrious Johnson at Charlie's Combat Club, and holds a third-degree black belt in taekwondo. He's a former CFFC flyweight champion with 8 KO/TKO wins (including a spinning wheel kick KO in 2024), 1 submission and 1 decision. He stopped Mridul Saikia on the Contender Series on August 11, then knocked out Nilson Rojas in the second round of his UFC debut on August 29, 18 days later. Data caution: UFC.com's 9.01 strikes per minute and 3.65 knockdowns per 15 come from one UFC fight.

Gurule (11-4, age 32): A 5'5" Good Guys MMA striker and former Fury FC champion with a Colorado high school wrestling background. He's a volume puncher (5.44 landed per minute, 75% to the head) who takes a lot back (5.35 absorbed). He's 1-4 in the UFC: a TKO loss to Ode' Osbourne, decision losses to Jesus Aguilar and Alden Coria, a decision win over Daniel Barez on May 16, then an RNC loss to Rei Tsuruya two weeks later.

How it plays out: Hasan's kicking variety and finishing power against a durable but hittable pressure striker.

Pick: Hasan by KO/TKO. −1000 is parlay material only.

Prelims

Stephen Thompson (−151) vs Charles Radtke (+131), welterweight

Thompson (17-9-1, age 43): "Wonderboy" is the oldest fighter on the UFC roster and has said this will be his last fight (MMA News). He's a karate point-fighter (57-0 in kickboxing) with a 75" reach who fights from a bladed stance, kicks from range and counters: 4.05 landed per minute against 3.00 absorbed, 55% strike defense and 63% takedown defense, with 90% of his work standing. He has 8 KO/TKO, 1 submission and 8 decisions. Recent: an RNC loss to Shavkat Rakhmonov (December 2023), a third-round knockout loss to Joaquin Buckley (October 2024) and a split-decision loss to Gabriel Bonfim (July 2025).

Radtke (12-5, age 36): "Chuck Buffalo" trains at Valley Flow Striking and is 5-2 in the UFC: a debut decision over Mike Diamond, stoppages of Gilbert Urbina and Matthew Semelsberger, knockout losses to Carlos Prates and Mike Malott, an RNC of Daniel Frunza in November 2025 and a decision over Francisco Prado in April. He has 5 KO/TKO wins, 3 submissions and 4 decisions. Data caution: UFC.com's rate stats for Radtke cover only 16 significant strikes, which doesn't match his seven UFC fights, so I'm not using them.

How it plays out: Thompson at distance has given every welterweight problems; Radtke has to walk him down, close the gap and make it a brawl or a scramble, where his finishing ability shows up.

Pick: Thompson by decision.

Drew Dober (+165) vs Chris Duncan (−190), lightweight

Dober (29-15, age 38): A Muay Thai power puncher out of Pound 4 Pound with 17 KO/TKO wins, 5 submissions and 7 decisions; he hasn't won by decision since 2018. He lands 4.48 per minute and absorbs 4.33, with 53% strike defense and 58% takedown defense. He was stopped by Jean Silva (2024) and Manuel Torres (March 2025), then rebounded with stoppages of Kyle Prepolec and Michael Johnson in March.

Duncan (15-3, age 33): "The Problem" is a Scottish ATT fighter (UFC.com: Striker) with a dangerous front-headlock game: 7 KO/TKO, 4 submissions (three guillotines and an anaconda) and 4 decisions. He lands 4.83 per minute, averages 2.92 takedowns per 15 minutes, and has 51% strike defense. Recent: guillotines of Bolaji Oki and Jordan Vucenic, a decision over Mateusz Rebecki, an anaconda of Terrance McKinney in December, then a second-round RNC loss to Renato Moicano in April.

How it plays out: Dober's power against Duncan's chokes. Dober wants a standing fight in the pocket; Duncan can trade, but his edge is the takedown and front headlock whenever Dober ducks in.

Pick: Duncan by submission or decision.

Jim Miller (+174) vs Terrance McKinney (−200), lightweight

Miller (39-19, age 42): "A-10" fought at UFC 100, 200 and 300 and is a jiu-jitsu black belt with 22 submission wins (six guillotines, seven RNCs, four armbars and more), 7 KO/TKO and 10 decisions. He's not a volume striker (2.92 landed per minute, 57% strike defense, 48% takedown defense), but he averages 1.72 submission attempts per 15 minutes and is lethal in scrambles. Recent: a guillotine of Damon Jackson, a decision loss to Chase Hooper, and a first-round guillotine of Jared Gordon in May.

McKinney (18-9, age 31): "T. Wrecks" holds the fastest knockout in UFC lightweight history (seven seconds) and has never won a decision: 9 KO/TKO, 9 submissions, 17 first-round finishes, and an average fight time of 2:45. He lands 6.83 per minute, averages 3.27 takedowns and 1.82 submission attempts per 15, but his 43% strike defense is the flip side, and he's been finished early too. Recent: an anaconda loss to Chris Duncan in December, a 24-second TKO of Kyle Nelson in March, then a first-round stoppage loss to King Green in July.

How it plays out: McKinney's first two minutes are the most dangerous on the prelims; Miller's guillotine is waiting the moment McKinney shoots or ducks. If Miller survives the opening burst, the fight swings toward him.

Pick: McKinney by first-round finish. If you like chaos, Miller at +174 is the submission-upset play.

Donte Johnson (−175) vs César Almeida (+153), middleweight

Johnson (9-0, age 27): "Lock Jaw" turned pro in August 2024 and is out of Gnosis Combat Sports, with a 74" reach on a 5'8" frame. He has 7 KO/TKO wins, 1 submission (guillotine) and 1 decision. In the UFC: a guillotine of Sedriques Dumas, a decision over Cody Brundage in March, and a first-round knockout of Eric McConico in August. He lands 3.36 per minute, absorbs 2.11, has 61% strike defense and 77% takedown defense. Data caution: three UFC fights.

Almeida (7-3, age 38): "Cesinha" is a Brazilian striker (UFC.com: Striker) with 5 KO/TKO wins and 2 decisions. He's accurate (60%) but hittable (44% strike defense, 53% takedown defense). He's lost two straight: a decision to Cezary Oleksiejczuk in December 2025 and a first-round knockout by Damian Pinas in July.

How it plays out: Johnson's youth, takedown threat and power against a veteran striker coming off a knockout loss.

Pick: Johnson by KO/TKO.

Early prelims

Jared Gordon (+315) vs Melquizael Costa (−415), lightweight

Gordon (21-9, 1 NC, age 37): "Flash" is a pressure boxer-wrestler out of Kill Cliff FC: 5.57 landed per minute, 74% of it to the head, 1.91 takedowns per 15 minutes, 55% strike defense. He has 8 KO/TKO, 2 submissions and 11 decisions. Recent: a knockout of Thiago Moises in May 2025, then a stoppage loss to Rafa Garcia and a first-round guillotine loss to Jim Miller in May.

Costa (26-7, age 30): "The Dalmatian," No. 12 at featherweight on UFC.com, is a Chute Boxe finisher moving up to lightweight, with 9 KO/TKO, 8 submissions (including two heel hooks) and 9 decisions. UFC.com credits him with six wins in his last seven, including a decision over Julian Erosa and first-round stoppages of Morgan Charriere and Dan Ige; his most recent fight was a five-round decision loss to Arnold Allen in May. He lands 4.29 per minute with 50% strike defense and 51% takedown defense, and spreads his strikes around (30% body, 23% legs).

How it plays out: Costa has the bigger arsenal and the momentum; Gordon needs volume and takedowns to make it ugly.

Pick: Costa by KO/TKO.

Macy Chiasson (+204) vs Bia Mesquita (−246), women's bantamweight

Chiasson (11-6, age 35): The TUF 28 winner (No. 9 on the event page) is a 5'11" Fortis MMA fighter with a 72" reach who does a lot of her work in the clinch (31% of her significant strikes). She has 4 KO/TKO, 3 submissions and 4 decisions. She's lost three straight decisions (Ketlen Vieira, Yana Santos, Ailin Perez in February) after finishing Pannie Kianzad and Mayra Bueno Silva in 2024.

Mesquita (8-0, age 35): "The Lady GOAT" (No. 11) is a 10-time IBJJF world champion, an ADCC champion and a fourth-degree black belt out of ATT. Her MMA game is the takedown and the back: 3.57 takedowns per 15 minutes, 81% of her significant strikes landed on the ground, and 6 submission wins (five RNCs, one armbar) plus 1 KO/TKO. She's 3-0 in the UFC, all by submission: Irina Alekseeva, Montse Rendon and Melissa Mullins (armbar, June). Data caution: three UFC fights, all short.

How it plays out: Chiasson's size and clinch work against the most decorated grappler in the division. Chiasson's 63% takedown defense has to hold, and she has to make Mesquita strike.

Pick: Mesquita by submission.

Alatengheili (+325) vs Juan Diaz (−425), bantamweight

Alatengheili (18-10-2, age 34): "The Mongolian Knight," from China's Inner Mongolia region, steps in for the injured Adrian Yanez. UFC.com lists his style as Wrestling, but 96% of his significant strikes come standing; he lands 2.71 per minute at 32% accuracy and absorbs 4.96. He has 5 KO/TKO, 3 submissions and 10 decisions, and 75% takedown defense. He won a split decision over John Castaneda on September 26, seven weeks before this fight.

Diaz (16-1-1, age 28): "Pegajoso" is a Peruvian grinder out of the UFC Performance Institute in Mexico City, on a nine-fight win streak, a former LUX Fight League bantamweight champion with 5 KO/TKO, 3 submissions and 8 decisions. He submitted Malcolm Wellmaker with a rear-naked choke in the second round of his UFC debut in May. Data caution: one UFC fight; UFC.com's 5.50 takedowns per 15 minutes come from that one fight.

How it plays out: Diaz wants to wrestle and take the back; Alatengheili is durable and rarely finished, so the question is method more than winner.

Pick: Diaz by decision.

Evan Elder (−101) vs Samuel Sanches (−119), lightweight

Elder (10-2, age 29): "The Phenom" trains at the UFC Performance Institute and is a busy kickboxer (5.66 landed per minute, 92% standing, 38% to the body) with 4 KO/TKO, 2 submissions and 4 decisions. He's on a three-fight win streak (decisions over Genaro Valdez and Gauge Young, an arm-triangle of Darrius Flowers), but he hasn't fought since April 2025, and his 40% takedown defense is a weak spot.

Sanches (11-1, age 23): "The Prodigy" makes his UFC debut out of Galpão da Luta with a 74.5" reach, 8 KO/TKO wins and 1 submission, and a 9-0 record at lightweight. He knocked out Chasen Blair on the Contender Series in September 2025, then withdrew from a March UFC debut with an arm injury, so this is his first fight in 431 days (UFC.com). Data caution: no UFC fights; UFC.com's numbers come from one Contender Series bout.

How it plays out: The market can't separate them. Sanches has the reach and the finishing record; Elder has the UFC experience and the pace, but he's been out even longer.

Pick: Sanches, narrowly. It's close to a coin flip.

Data gaps

UFC.com's rate stats for Radtke look incomplete and weren't used. Nunes hasn't fought since June 2023 and Elder since April 2025, so their numbers are from older fights. UFC.com's athlete-record widget labels every recent result as a win, so I used the written fight-by-fight list in each bio. Rankings are UFC.com event-page numbers; a few athlete pages show slightly different numbers (Medić No. 11, Chiasson No. 10), and other trackers differ. UFC.com lists Costa at 26-7; some outlets list 26-8. Weigh-ins are Friday, so misses aren't reflected here.
