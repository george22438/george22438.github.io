UFC Qatar Procházka vs. Stirling: BetOnline Moneylines + Fight-by-Fight Analysis

George Garcia · Nov 19, 2026

UFC Fight Night: Procházka vs. Stirling (UFC Qatar / UFC Fight Night 294): Saturday, November 21, 2026, ABHA Arena, Doha, Qatar. It's a morning card in the U.S.: prelims 7:00 AM PT, main card 10:00 AM PT, all on Paramount+. Twelve fights: five on the main card and seven prelims, including a late-added lightweight bout between UFC newcomers John Mitchell and Shoaib Yousaf. Former light heavyweight champion Jiří Procházka (No. 2 on UFC.com's event page) headlines against unbeaten Navajo Stirling (No. 5) over five rounds, and Aljamain Sterling (No. 4) meets Kevin Vallejos (No. 9) in a featherweight co-main that Heavy also lists at five rounds.

Main card

Jiří Procházka (+152) vs Navajo Stirling (−179), light heavyweight, 5 rounds

Procházka (32-6-1, age 34): The former champion out of Jetsaam Gym is listed by UFC.com as a karate stylist, and he fights like it: unorthodox angles, low hands and a constant hunt for the finish. He has 28 KO/TKO wins, 3 submissions and exactly one decision (September 2016), with 23 first-round finishes. His UFC numbers show both sides of that: 5.61 significant strikes landed per minute against 5.67 absorbed, 56% accuracy, and only 45% strike defense; 77% of his strikes go to the head. His only UFC losses are three title fights: two stoppages by Alex Pereira (November 2023, June 2024) and a first-round knockout by Carlos Ulberg for the vacant title at UFC 327 on April 11, his last fight. Between those he stopped Aleksandar Rakić, Jamahal Hill and Khalil Rountree Jr., the last two in the third round (UFC.com).

Stirling (11-0, age 29): A 6'4" New Zealand kickboxer (UFC.com: Kickboxer) out of City Kickboxing, the same gym as Ulberg, the champion who knocked Procházka out. He came through Season 8 of the Contender Series and opened 3-0 in the UFC with three decisions (Tuco Tokkos, Ivan Erslan, Rodolfo Bellato), then turned into a finisher in 2026: stoppages of Bruno Lopes and Ion Cutelaba in the second round and Jan Błachowicz in 2:56 on August 1. His sheet is the cleaner one: 6.47 landed per minute against 2.56 absorbed, 55% accuracy, 58% strike defense and 71% takedown defense, and he spreads it out, with 31% of his significant strikes to the body and 18% to the legs. He has 7 KO/TKO wins and 4 decisions. This is his first UFC main event and his first five-round fight in the UFC.

How it plays out: Procházka has a one-inch reach edge (80" to 79") and far more experience at the top, but he absorbs more than he lands, and Stirling is the more disciplined, body-first kickboxer who comes from the same room that just solved him. Stirling wants distance, body kicks and clean counters; Procházka wants chaos, a broken rhythm and a firefight, where his finishing record speaks for itself. Five rounds in the UFC is new ground for Stirling, while Procházka has won a title in the fifth round.

Pick: Stirling by decision or late TKO. At +152, Procházka's power and unpredictability make him a live underdog.

Aljamain Sterling (+113) vs Kevin Vallejos (−132), featherweight

Sterling (26-5, age 37): "Funk Master" is the former bantamweight champion, now 3-1 at featherweight, out of Serra-Longo (UFC.com). He's a two-time Division III All-American wrestler, and his game is relentless grappling: 2.54 takedowns per 15 minutes, 0.64 submission attempts per 15, and 8 submissions (five rear-naked chokes) among his wins. He's also hard to hit (2.15 absorbed per minute, 59% strike defense) and works the body hard: 31% of his significant strikes land there. He has 0.00 knockdowns per 15 and 15 decision wins, and his takedown defense is 45%. At 145 he lost a decision to Movsar Evloev in December 2024, then won five-round decisions over Brian Ortega (August 2025, at a 153-pound catchweight after Ortega's weight-cut trouble, per Wikipedia) and Youssef Zalal (April 25).

Vallejos (18-1, age 24): "El Chino" is an Argentine striker out of Team Lucas Mineiro on a seven-fight win streak; his only pro loss was a Contender Series decision to Jean Silva in September 2023 (UFC.com). He's 4-0 in the UFC: a first-round TKO of Choi Seung-woo, a decision over Danny Silva, a spinning-backfist knockout of Giga Chikadze in December 2025, and a first-round TKO of Josh Emmett on March 14 (Wikipedia). He has 13 KO/TKO wins and 2 RNCs, lands 6.12 per minute with 1.65 knockdowns per 15, and has 85% takedown defense. The trade-off: 4.41 absorbed per minute and 48% accuracy.

How it plays out: UFC.com calls it a classic grappler-versus-striker fight. Vallejos has the power and the youth; Sterling has the wrestling, the experience in long fights and the defensive striking numbers. The question is whether Vallejos's 85% takedown defense holds against the best grappler he's ever faced. If it stays standing, Vallejos is the bigger threat; if Sterling gets to the back, it's his kind of fight.

Pick: Vallejos by KO/TKO, narrowly. Sterling at +113 is a reasonable underdog play if you trust the wrestling.

Dan Hooker (−187) vs Brian Ortega (+158), lightweight

Hooker (24-15, age 36): "The Hangman," No. 14 on the event page, is a 6'0" New Zealand striker with a 75" reach who has fought everywhere from featherweight to heavyweight (UFC.com). He's an all-action volume fighter: 4.82 landed per minute against 4.94 absorbed, 49% strike defense, 77% takedown defense, and a heavy leg-kick game (20% of his significant strikes go to the legs). He has 11 KO/TKO wins, 7 submissions (including three guillotines) and 6 decisions. He comes in on a three-fight losing streak: an arm-triangle loss to Arman Tsarukyan in Doha last November, then TKO losses to Benoît Saint Denis at UFC 325 and Salahdine Parnasse on September 5 (Wikipedia). Before that he won split decisions over Jalin Turner and Mateusz Gamrot.

Ortega (16-5, 1 NC, age 35): "T-City" is a jiu-jitsu fighter (UFC.com: Jiu-Jitsu) and two-time featherweight title challenger making his lightweight debut. He's a submission threat from anywhere: 8 submissions (four triangles, an arm triangle, an RNC, two guillotines), 3 KO/TKO and 5 decisions, with six third-round finishes. The standup numbers are the concern: 3.84 landed per minute at 37% accuracy against 6.38 absorbed, with 49% strike defense. He's lost two straight, decisions to Diego Lopes (September 2024) and Aljamain Sterling (August 2025), and hasn't fought in 15 months; his last win was an arm-triangle of Yair Rodríguez in February 2024.

How it plays out: Hooker has six inches of reach and the busier striking; Ortega has the far better grappling and the late-round submission game. Hooker's danger is getting dragged into scrambles; Ortega's is eating clean shots for three rounds while he looks for one. UFC.com's preview calls Ortega "a giant question mark" after the layoff and the move up.

Pick: Hooker by decision. Ortega at +158 only gets there with a submission.

Kyoji Horiguchi (−195) vs Asu Almabayev (+165), flyweight

Horiguchi (36-6, age 36): "Karate Kid," No. 5, is the American Top Team veteran who fought Demetrious Johnson for the flyweight title in 2015 and went 16-3, 1 NC away from the UFC (wins over Kai Asakura and Manel Kape) before returning (UFC.com). He's a karate-based, in-and-out striker: 3.64 landed per minute against just 2.08 absorbed, 64% strike defense, and 1.67 takedowns per 15 to mix it up. He has 15 KO/TKO wins, 6 submissions and 15 decisions. Back in the UFC he's 2-1: an RNC of Tagir Ulanbekov in Doha last November, a decision over Amir Albazi in February, then a third-round TKO loss to Manel Kape on June 20.

Almabayev (24-3, age 32): "Zulfikar," No. 7, is a Kazakh grappler out of Dar Team (UFC.com: Jiu-Jitsu) who has won 20 of his last 21 and is 7-1 in the UFC. The numbers are pure grappling: 4.74 takedowns per 15 minutes and 1.44 submission attempts per 15, with 11 submissions (six RNCs, two guillotines, a Peruvian necktie, an armbar) among his wins. His striking output is low (2.28 landed per minute) but so is what he absorbs (1.94). His takedown defense is 44%. He's on a three-fight win streak, most recently a guillotine of Alex Perez in Doha last November and a Suloev stretch of Charles Johnson in June; his lone UFC loss is a third-round TKO by Kape in March 2025.

How it plays out: Horiguchi wants range, speed and blitzes in and out; Almabayev wants to close distance, chain takedowns and grind. Horiguchi has a four-inch reach edge (69" to 65"), but his 61% takedown defense will be tested by one of the division's most persistent wrestlers. Both have lost to Kape, and UFC.com frames this as a possible title eliminator.

Pick: Horiguchi by decision. Almabayev at +165 is a real threat if the takedowns land early.

Jared Cannonier (+239) vs Ikram Aliskerov (−290), middleweight

Cannonier (18-10, age 42): "Tha Killa Gorilla," No. 12, is a former title challenger out of ABJJ + Gracie Barra Alaska (UFC.com: Striker) who turns 43 in March. He's a patient counter-puncher with power and a heavy leg-kick game: 4.25 landed per minute against 4.36 absorbed, 56% strike defense, 65% takedown defense, and 81% of his work standing. He has 11 KO/TKO, 2 submissions and 5 decisions. He has one win in his last five: a TKO loss to Nassourdine Imavov, a decision loss to Caio Borralho, a fourth-round TKO of Gregory Rodrigues in February 2025, then decision losses to Michael "Venom" Page and Christian Leroy Duncan (July 18) (Wikipedia).

Aliskerov (18-2, age 33): A Dagestani multiple-time world sambo champion (per UFC.com) out of the Nurmagomedov School, No. 13 (UFC.com: Sambo). He's 5-1 in the UFC with three straight wins: a first-round TKO of Andre Muniz, then decisions over JunYong Park and Brunno Ferreira in June. His lone UFC loss was a first-round knockout by Robert Whittaker on a week's notice. He's an aggressive striker-wrestler: 5.56 landed per minute at 61% accuracy, 1.04 knockdowns per 15, 3.82 takedowns per 15, and nobody has taken him down in the UFC (100% takedown defense). The soft spot is 45% strike defense.

How it plays out: Aliskerov has the pace, the wrestling and nine years on his opponent. Cannonier still has the power and the counter game, and UFC.com notes Aliskerov "hasn't always been the most defensively responsible." If Cannonier lands clean as Aliskerov pressures, the +239 cashes; otherwise expect takedowns and a pace he hasn't matched lately.

Pick: Aliskerov by decision or late TKO.

Prelims

Aleksandre Topuria (−270) vs Santiago Luna (+224), bantamweight

Topuria (7-1, age 30): "El Conquistador," the older brother of former two-division champion Ilia Topuria, fights out of Team Topuria (UFC.com: Boxing) and has been a BJJ black belt since 2018. He's 2-0 in the UFC, both decisions (Colby Thicknesse, Bekzat Almakhan), and on a five-fight win streak, but he hasn't fought since November 2025 in Doha. He has 3 KO/TKO and 2 submissions. Data caution: his UFC.com rates (2.93 landed, 2.57 absorbed, 69% strike defense, 1.50 takedowns per 15) come from two fights.

Luna (8-1, age 22): "BorderBoy" out of San Diego knocked out Quang Le in 2:48 in his September 2025 debut, beat Angel Pacheco by decision in February, then took a short-notice fight with Bryce Mitchell and was submitted by arm-triangle at 4:52 of the third round in June (UFC.com). He has 4 RNC wins and 3 KO/TKOs, and a 74" reach, six inches longer than Topuria's. His UFC rates: 4.74 landed per minute, 68% strike defense, 2.30 takedowns per 15. Data caution: three UFC fights.

How it plays out: Topuria is the more experienced grappler and the steadier boxer; Luna has the reach, the volume and the youth. Topuria's year away is the unknown.

Pick: Topuria by decision.

Shamil Gaziev (−195) vs Tallison Teixeira (+165), heavyweight

Gaziev (15-3, age 36): A Dagestan-born heavyweight who represents Bahrain (UFC.com: MMA), out of KHK Team, with 10 KO/TKO wins, 3 RNCs and five finishes in under a minute. He's 4-3 in the UFC and finishes or gets finished: a 56-second stoppage of Martin Buday, a TKO (retirement) loss to Jairzinho Rozenstruik, a decision over Don'Tale Mayes, a KO of Thomas Petersen, first-round and second-round KO losses to Waldo Cortes-Acosta and Brando Peričić, and a first-round KO of Kennedy Nzechukwu in August. His defense is the issue: 2.90 landed against 4.86 absorbed per minute, 45% strike defense, though his takedown defense is 83%.

Teixeira (9-2, age 26): "Xicao" is a 6'7" Brazilian striker out of Gor MMA with an 83" reach and 7 KO/TKO wins, all eight of his finishes in the first round (UFC.com). He's alternated results in the UFC: a 35-second stoppage of Justin Tafa, a 35-second TKO loss to Derrick Lewis, a decision over Tai Tuivasa in January, then a 39-second TKO loss to Sergei Pavlovich in May. Data caution: four UFC fights, two of them under a minute.

How it plays out: Two heavyweights with real power and leaky defense. Teixeira has 4.5 inches of reach; Gaziev has more experience and wrestling as a backup. Early finish likely either way, and BetOnline's total of 1.5 rounds leans that way too.

Pick: Gaziev by first-round KO/TKO.

Amir Albazi (−104) vs Alessandro Costa (−112), flyweight

Albazi (17-3, age 33): "The Prince," No. 9, is an Iraqi-born jiu-jitsu fighter out of Great Britain Top Team (UFC.com) with 9 submissions (five RNCs, three kimuras, a triangle) and 5 KO/TKO wins. He hasn't won since a debated split decision over Kai Kara-France in June 2023; he lost a five-round decision to Brandon Moreno in November 2024 and a decision to Kyoji Horiguchi in February (Wikipedia). His striking rates are low: 2.47 landed per minute at 34% accuracy against 3.88 absorbed, 59% strike defense, 63% takedown defense.

Costa (17-5, age 30): "Nono" is a Brazilian jiu-jitsu-based finisher (UFC.com) with 7 KO/TKO and 7 submissions (four armbars). He's on a three-fight stoppage run this year: Stewart Nicoll (April), Matt Schnell (June) and Cody Durden by RNC at UFC 329 on July 11. He has 87% takedown defense and 0.88 knockdowns per 15. His UFC losses: a third-round KO by Albazi in December 2022 (he took that fight as a replacement), a decision to Steve Erceg and a TKO by Alden Coria.

How it plays out: A rematch of a fight Albazi won by third-round knockout almost four years ago. Since then their paths have crossed: Costa has been finishing people, Albazi hasn't won in more than two years. The market has it as a coin flip.

Pick: Costa, narrowly. It's the closest fight on the board.

Jake Matthews (−241) vs Tahir Abdullayev (+201), welterweight

Matthews (23-8, age 32): "The Celtic Kid" out of Australian Elite Team is making his 25th UFC walk (UFC.com). He's a well-rounded veteran: 3.36 landed against 2.58 absorbed per minute, 61% strike defense, 67% takedown defense, and 9 submissions (seven RNCs), 9 decisions and 5 KO/TKOs. He's won four of his last five: decisions over Phil Rowe and Francisco Prado, a submission of Chidi Njokuani, a D'Arce loss to Neil Magny, then a decision over Carlston Harris in May.

Abdullayev (20-3, age 29): "Tank" is a former UAE Warriors welterweight champion from Azerbaijan with 11 KO/TKO wins, 7 submissions, and only one finish loss (an arm triangle in March 2018) (UFC.com). He stopped Jefferson Nascimento at 4:28 of the third round in his UFC debut in Baku in June. Data caution: one UFC fight; UFC.com's 51% ground striking and 2.07 takedowns per 15 come from that bout alone.

How it plays out: Matthews has far more experience, a slick back-take game and good defense; Abdullayev is a finisher who pressured for a late stoppage in his debut. Matthews wins most of the scrambles if it gets there.

Pick: Matthews by decision.

Nikolas Motta (+147) vs Dom Mar Fan (−173), lightweight

Motta (15-6, 1 NC, age 32): "Iron" is a Brazilian kickboxer out of Bangtao Muay Thai and MMA (UFC.com: Kickboxer) with 10 KO/TKO wins and no submissions. He's 3-3, 1 NC in the UFC: stoppages of Cameron VanCamp and Tom Nolan, a decision over Maheshate, and stoppage losses to Jim Miller, Manuel Torres and Nazim Sadykhov. That Sadykhov fight in June 2025 earned Fight of the Night, and he hasn't fought since. He's a head-hunter (79% of his significant strikes go to the head) with 1.13 knockdowns per 15, but he absorbs 5.28 per minute against 4.13 landed.

Mar Fan (9-3, age 26): "Street Buddha" is an Australian out of City Kickboxing who won Season 4 of Road to UFC at lightweight with a decision over SangWook Kim at UFC 325, then was submitted by Kody Steele's heel hook in the first round in May (UFC.com). He's a clinch-and-grapple fighter: 29% of his significant strikes come in the clinch and 23% on the ground, with 1.84 takedowns per 15. He has 1 KO, 3 submissions and 5 decisions. Data caution: one official UFC fight plus the tournament final.

How it plays out: Motta wants a striking fight at range; Mar Fan wants to clinch and wrestle. Motta's 83% takedown defense against Mar Fan's grinding game, after 17 months off, is the fight.

Pick: Mar Fan by decision.

Diyar Nurgozhay (−235) vs Alik Lorenz (+197), light heavyweight

Nurgozhay (12-2, age 29): A Kazakh Contender Series graduate out of Tobet Top Team and a 2021 GAMMA amateur world champion (UFC.com) with 7 KO/TKO wins and five wins in 1:06 or less. He's 2-2 in the UFC: submission losses to Brendson Ribeiro (kimura) and Uran Satybaldiev (Ezekiel), then a decision over Rafael Tobias in March and a first-round stoppage of Bruno Lopes in August. He's a body-and-leg kicker: 37% of his significant strikes go to the body and 27% to the legs, at 61% accuracy and 60% strike defense.

Lorenz (8-2, age 29): A Davenport, Iowa, striker who trains at The MMA Lab with Jared Cannonier (UFC.com), making his UFC debut. He has 7 KO/TKO wins and 1 RNC, and has been out of the first round only once as a pro. He was stopped in 30 seconds by Abdul Rakhman Yakhyaev on Season 9 of the Contender Series, then earned his contract this year with a first-round stoppage of Mahamed Aly. Data caution: UFC.com's numbers (including 29% strike defense) come from Contender Series bouts only.

How it plays out: Two first-round finishers. Nurgozhay has the more varied kicking game and UFC experience; Lorenz has power and nothing to lose. Both of Nurgozhay's losses are submissions, which isn't Lorenz's game.

Pick: Nurgozhay by KO/TKO.

John Mitchell (−128) vs Shoaib Yousaf (+110), lightweight

Mitchell (11-3, age 31): "The Cork Horse" is an Irish lightweight who has fought for Cage Warriors, UAE Warriors and five times for the PFL, mostly on PFL Europe cards (MMA Fighting). He lost a unanimous decision to Makkasharip Zaynukov at PFL Champions Series 2 in 2025, then rebounded with a knockout at Cage Conflict 18 in July. UFC.com credits him with six finishes, and Heavy notes he has also boxed professionally. He was preparing for a November 27 fight in Dubai before taking this one (MMA Junkie).

Yousaf (14-3, age 31): An English former IMMAF world champion and the FightStar Championship lightweight champion, with stops in Brave CF, ACB and Oktagon (MMA Fighting, MMA Junkie). He's on a six-fight win streak, and his last three wins are first-round knockouts (UFC.com), most recently a stoppage of Tariel Abbasov at FCC 47 in September.

How it plays out: Two late-notice UFC debuts with no UFC data. Yousaf has the longer streak and recent first-round power; Mitchell has fought the higher level of opposition in the PFL. Short notice and the Octagon debut make this the hardest fight on the card to read.

Pick: Yousaf, as a small underdog. Low confidence.
