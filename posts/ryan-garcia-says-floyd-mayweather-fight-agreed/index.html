Ryan Garcia Says He and Floyd Mayweather Agreed to Fight. Nothing Is Signed Yet.

George Garcia · Oct 21, 2026

The callout, the claim, and what's actually real: On Wednesday, October 21, 2026, WBC welterweight champion Ryan Garcia posted on X that he and Floyd Mayweather have agreed to fight, at 147 pounds, for Ryan's WBC title, with Floyd's 50-0 on the line. Floyd reposted it. That's it so far. No contract has been announced, no date, no venue, and Uncrowned reported that no serious negotiations had happened as of Wednesday. Until there's paper, this is Ryan's word plus a Floyd repost.

What was actually said

At 2:36 PM PT on October 21, Ryan posted on X:

"Trust me at first I thought this was bull***
But it's real Floyd and I agree to a fight
147 for the WBC welterweight championship of the world
50-0 vs The current champ"

On Instagram, per Yahoo Sports / Uncrowned, he added: "Just got off the phone with @floydmayweather. Talking about fighting - it's real and he wants to do a real fight 147. He and I both agreed to post this! Let's Rumble." Bleacher Report reported he also shared a photo of the two of them on FaceTime.

Floyd's side: About half an hour later, @FloydMayweather reposted Ryan's post. Yahoo reported Floyd had not commented publicly in his own words, and sources told Uncrowned's Darshan Desai that no serious negotiations had taken place.

How we got here: On October 7, after FightHype reported talks, Ryan answered on X (via BoxingScene): "Okay Floyd let's see how much you love that zero... And no I wouldn't do an exhibition, I want his record on the f***ing line... I'm ready Floyd, remember you made this call. I'm just returning the favor. Offer is on the table." On October 20, WBC president Mauricio Sulaiman told Hard Rock Bet (via FightBook MMA) that "if Floyd Mayweather passes all the examinations, if Floyd Mayweather gets a licence from a boxing commission where the fight is to be taking place, the WBC will certainly support Floyd Mayweather."

The status board:

  • Signed? No contract has been reported.
  • Officially announced? No. There's no promoter or network announcement. It's a fighter's post and a repost.
  • Pro or exhibition? Ryan says pro, for the WBC 147-pound title, and he has said he won't do an exhibition. Floyd would still need a commission license and the WBC's sign-off.
  • Date / venue? None reported.
  • Weight: 147, per Ryan.
  • Odds: I couldn't find a BetOnline line on this fight, so there are no odds here.

The tale of the tape

Floyd Mayweather (50-0, 27 KOs), 49 years old. His last pro fight was the 2017 win over Conor McGregor. He's done eight exhibitions since. A planned late-2026 pro rematch with Manny Pacquiao fell apart, and BoxingScene reports he hasn't fought at welterweight in 11 years.

Ryan Garcia (26-2, 21 KOs), 28 years old. He won the WBC welterweight title from Mario Barrios in February and defended it with a second-round knockout of Conor Benn in September. (Records and ages via Yahoo Sports / Uncrowned and BoxingScene.)

Two generations, one squared ring

This is a great picture of two generations meeting in the squared ring. Floyd is the old school, cut from a different cloth: he came up before the internet and social media, when celebrity meant old-school fame and the cameras in his face belonged to an HBO production crew. Ryan is the current generation, with unlimited information and unlimited contacts and connections. He's been an online socialite basically his whole life, and as a modern-day celebrity he's used to a streamer's camera in his face.

In the ring, it's The Best Ever against the most polarizing figure in boxing. Does Floyd still have that Cobra spine that lets him lean back like the Matrix out of punches, or has age turned his vertebrae to rust? Will Ryan keep dominating, prove the miserable, jealous haters wrong, and send his pride into further lift-off with a KO? Or will he swing and miss at thin air like the other 50 opponents...

Style breakdown

Floyd Mayweather: defense first, counter everything

Style: A defensive counterpuncher, and one of the best ring generals ever. He controls range and tempo, rarely stays in front of you, and wins rounds by making you miss and then making you pay.

Defense: The Philly shell / shoulder roll. His lead shoulder is high, his right hand guards his chin, and his left arm sits low across his body. He rolls right hands off that lead shoulder and leans back out of range.

Signature punches: The pull counter (he leans back off your jab and comes forward with a straight right), the right hand (the lead right that's been his best weapon his whole career), and the check hook, a left hook he throws while pivoting away from a fighter charging in, which is how he knocked out Ricky Hatton.

Ryan Garcia: speed and the left hook

Style: An orthodox offensive boxer-puncher with some of the fastest hands in the sport. He likes to set traps and end fights with one shot, and 21 of his 26 wins are knockouts.

Defense: He leans on speed, reflexes and distance more than a tight shell. His defense is mostly reaction time and getting his own shot off first.

Signature punches: The left hook. It's the punch that put Devin Haney down three times in 2024 and knocked out Luke Campbell with a body shot. His hand speed sets everything else up, a quick jab and right hand, with the hook coming in behind them.

Where it meets

Floyd's whole defense is built to beat aggressive, fast-handed punchers who commit, and the check hook is made for the guy who charges in. Ryan's left hook is built to catch fighters at the exact moment they come forward or step in. The real question is the 21-year age gap and nine years since Floyd's last real pro fight. That's a question only an actual fight can answer, and right now there isn't a signed one.
