Zuffa Boxing 11: BetOnline Moneylines + Fight-by-Fight Analysis

George Garcia · Sep 25, 2026

Zuffa Boxing 11: Fisher vs Pirotton — Saturday, September 26, 2026, Copper Box Arena, Stratford, London. Prelims 9:15 AM PT, main card 11:00 AM PT on Paramount+ (US) and Sky Sports (UK).

BetOnline American moneylines first, then fight-by-fight style and how-they-win notes. Favorites in red, underdogs in green. Style depth follows what is verified; thin or debuting files stay honest.

Odds source: BetOnline.ag — checked September 25, 2026, ~1:28 PM PT. Lines move. Leon Hughes vs Michael Stephenson is scratched / off the official card and is not listed here.

Main card

Johnny Fisher (−1250) vs Michael Pirotton (+643) — Heavyweight, 10 rounds

Fisher: Aggressive pressure heavyweight who looks for the KO. He walks opponents down, crowds the pocket, and tries to end nights early rather than grind decisions.

Pirotton: Patient counter-puncher with footwork. He wants space, timing, and counters off the back foot instead of matching Fisher’s forward heat.

Matchup read: Fisher’s path is sustained pressure until the stoppage comes. Pirotton’s path is stay off the ropes, make Fisher miss, and cash a counter. BetOnline has Fisher as massive chalk (−1250).

Craig Richards (−135) vs Radivoje Kalajdzic (+105) — Light heavyweight, 10 rounds

Richards: Tall jab-and-outside boxer. He wins by range, left-hand control, and making the other man come through the stick.

Kalajdzic: Boxer-puncher who builds off the jab into a straight right. More willing to trade once he plants his feet.

Matchup read: Closest main-card number on this board. Richards wants the outside; Kalajdzic wants to close and fire the right. Note: BetOnline has Richards −135 while some books lean Kalajdzic — treat this as a live market, not a settled chalk.

Lee Cutler (−160) vs Louis Greene (+124) — (BOL lists Cutler vs Greene)

Cutler: Action-oriented boxer-fighter. He engages, keeps rounds busy, and looks comfortable when the fight turns into a scrap.

Greene: Aggressive forward pressure with body work. He walks in, digs downstairs, and tries to make it physical.

Matchup read: Style clash of who forces the terms. Cutler is the mild BetOnline favorite (−160); Greene’s plus money needs that body attack to slow Cutler’s output.

Alex MacMillan (−25000) vs Guillaume Azagier (+2000)

MacMillan: Early power finisher. The board treats him as extreme chalk (−25000) — the kind of number you usually see when the market expects a short night.

Azagier: Style file is thin / unverified beyond the listing. Honest read: plus-money flier only if MacMillan’s early power does not land clean.

Matchup read: Market is all-in on MacMillan ending it early. Do not invent a deeper Azagier map from this number alone.

Cesar Mateo Tapia (−250) vs Leo Ruiz (+189)

Tapia: Undefeated prospect. Verified style notes are thin beyond the undefeated prospect label — do not invent a guard/punch map.

Ruiz: Robert Garcia–camp pressure and volume. He wins by pace, forwards pressure, and making rounds uncomfortable.

Matchup read: Tapia is clear favorite (−250) on reputation/prospect status. Ruiz’s path is the known one: pressure volume that tests whether Tapia’s undefeated file holds up under heat.

Kayla Allen (−8000) vs Elisabeth Mronsz (+1270)

Allen: Pro debut with amateur pedigree. Extreme favorite (−8000) on BetOnline — market expects a mismatch opener for the debutante.

Mronsz: Listed 2-0; style notes are thin / unverified beyond the record and the huge dog price (+1270).

Matchup read: Board says Allen in a rout. Mronsz is lottery-ticket plus money unless Allen’s debut nerves show; there is not enough verified tape here to draw a punch-map edge.

Prelims

Rogelio Romero (−275) vs Aurel Ignat (+206)

Romero: Southpaw on a KO streak. He wins by landing the left-hand power that has been finishing fights.

Ignat: Tall puncher. Length and power are the known tools; defensive detail is thinner.

Matchup read: Romero favored (−275) to keep the KO run going. Ignat needs the height/power to keep Romero honest at range.

Alexis De La Cerda (−500) vs Cain Lewis (+340)

De La Cerda: Right-hand power is the clear weapon. He is a solid favorite (−500) because one clean right can rewrite a round.

Lewis: Durable, decision-oriented. He survives, lasts, and tries to win on work rate and toughness rather than highlight power.

Matchup read: De La Cerda wants the early or mid-fight right hand. Lewis’s path is weather the power and drag it deep on the cards.

Fred Pullen (−25000) vs Leonardo Faretina (+2000)

Pullen: Amateur-to-pro debut profile. Same extreme-chalk tier as MacMillan (−25000) — market expects a quick, one-sided debut.

Faretina: Journeyman side of the card. Style map is thin beyond that role; +2000 is the classic “if the debutant freezes” number.

Matchup read: Treat the line as market confidence in Pullen’s debut power/readiness, not as a detailed technical projection on Faretina.

Board numbers are BetOnline American moneylines only (snapshot ~1:28 PM PT, Sep 25, 2026). Style notes stick to verified traits; thin files (Tapia, Azagier, Allen, Mronsz, Pullen, Faretina, parts of Cutler/Ignat) are labeled as such — no invented records or punch maps.
